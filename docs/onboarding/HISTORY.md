# Follow the development history

This route is for understanding the journey: decisions, alternatives, failed attempts, corrections and the evidence available at each step. Historical records are part of the project, not disposable onboarding overhead.

| Question | Start with |
|---|---|
| How did the repository and native engine arrive here? | [Reorganization and recovery](../../REORGANIZATION.md). |
| What had been accepted at earlier milestones? | [Acceptance through #201](../history/acceptance-through-201.md) and [2026-11-05 snapshot](../history/agent-acceptance-2026-11-05.md). |
| Why does a particular contract have its current shape? | The owning contract's linked issue/PR and dated decisions; then follow its predecessor and successor links. |
| What actually happened in an experiment or CI run? | [Engine evidence](../../engine/tests/), with exact source/configuration pins, failures and contributor/hosted distinctions. |
| Which earlier designs and research informed the work? | [Documentation catalog](../documentation-map.md#evidence-upstream-and-archive-collections), [research](../../research/) and [archives](../../archive/). |

Trace a decision as question → proposal → evidence → disposition → later correction. Preserve the original record even when its conclusion changes. A cancelled run remains cancelled; a successful descendant can qualify the resulting source without changing that history.

Old “current” or “next” wording belongs to its dated context. Use [current state](CURRENT-STATE.md) if you also need today's queue. Git history retains earlier file locations and wording; ordinary moves and commits preserve authorship and ancestry.

You can stop when the decision's provenance and limits answer your question. No build or complete implementation-status read is required.
